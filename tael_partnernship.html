FIANZA×TAEL
Partnership Brief

Tael is the cash register.
Fianza is the bank that reads it.

DRAFT — 2026-07-14 · PRE-OUTREACH · GROUNDED IN CODEBASE SCAN

Tael answers "how does an autonomous agent get paid." It has no answer for what comes next.

  1. "What if the paying agent's wallet is empty?" — today, the payment just fails and the request dies.
  2. "What can an earning agent do with the revenue Tael settles for it?" — today, nothing; it's just USDC sitting in a wallet.

Fianza is both answers. We give buyer-side agents a credit line so they can always afford Tael's tolls, and we give seller-side agents revenue-based underwriting that turns the income Tael settles for them into borrowing power. Both run on the exact rails Tael already uses — x402 payments in Stellar USDC, testnet and mainnet. No bridge, no new chain, no new asset.

What each side is missing that the other has

Tael has a rich, verifiable revenue signal — every receipt.payer and tx hash it settles — but no credit product to do anything with it. Fianza has a credit product built precisely to score on-chain agent revenue, but needs a revenue source worth underwriting. Tael is a revenue-manufacturing machine. Neither can build the loop alone. Together it closes on one chain, one asset.

Grounded in their code

These claims are reconciled against a read-only architecture scan of the Tael repo (rahulsainlll/tael-protocol), not just its public docs. Where the code contradicts the pitch, we say so and turn it into an ask rather than paper over it.

Key facts the scan established: settled payments are bare Operation.payment USDC transfers with no memo or tag; SettlementReceipt carries { txHash, payer, network, settledAt } but not amount or asset; PaymentVerifier is a clean swappable interface (packages/payments/src/verify.ts:24-26); there is no client-side agent SDK; and it is testnet-only on a self-issued test USDC, with no app-level replay protection.

A

Credit as a drop-in verifier

Tael's payment flow has a silent assumption: the paying agent already holds USDC. The x402 step "agent signs a USDC transfer" simply fails against an empty wallet. Tael has no fallback — and, confirmed by the scan, no buyer-side client SDK at all; payment construction is left entirely to the caller, demonstrated only by an internal ops script.

There are two ways Fianza closes this, and the scan makes clear which is cleaner.

A1The drop-in verifier — headline

PaymentVerifier is a swappable interface at packages/payments/src/verify.ts:24-26. A Tael service operator can drop in a Fianza-backed verifier that accepts a payment drawn from an agent's credit line — no fork, no core change, one config line. This is the strongest integration surface Tael exposes: credit becomes a settlement backend the seller opts into, exactly the way they already choose mock vs. Stellar verifier.

A2The buyer-side client — payWithCredit

Fianza's SDK ships the missing client Tael doesn't have. Its flagship payWithCredit(url, price) does draw-on-402:

payWithCredit(url, price) balance ≥ price ? → pay over x402 balance < price ? → borrow the shortfall against the agent's line, THEN pay

The agent never "decides to borrow" — it just transacts, and the credit line silently covers what its cash can't. Fianza's demo agent already does exactly this against a generic x402 endpoint (agents/demo/plain.mjs), so pointing it at a Tael-wrapped route is close to zero new code.

A1 and A2 compose: A2 is what the agent runs, A1 is what the service trusts.

Pitch line

Tael turns any API into a paid API. Fianza makes sure the agent can always afford it.

B

Revenue-based underwriting for sellers

Think about what an agent that wraps its service in Tael is: a machine with a verifiable, on-chain, growing USDC revenue stream. Every payment Tael settles lands at that agent's Stellar payTo address, stamped on-chain with a tx hash.

That stream is exactly and only what Fianza's underwriter scores. Fianza's thesis is credit for agents based on real, verifiable, on-chain revenue. Tael is the machine that manufactures that revenue and writes it to the ledger Fianza already reads.

The one real blocker — and it's fixable

The scan found that Tael settles payments as bare Operation.payment USDC transfers with no memo or tag, and SettlementReceipt omits amount/asset. So from on-chain data alone, Fianza cannot distinguish Tael revenue from any other USDC hitting the same address, nor attribute a transfer to a specific invocation. Angle B is not free today.

The fix is small and lives entirely on Tael's side: attach a memo/tag convention to settlement payments, and add amount/asset to the receipt. That single change makes every Tael payment self-attributing and turns Fianza's underwriter into a drop-in reader. This is the central technical ask below.

The flywheel — once payments are attributable

Agent wraps its API with Tael and starts earning USDC from other agents. That revenue history is its creditworthiness — Fianza indexes the payTo address, sees consistent income, and underwrites a credit line against it. The agent borrows against future Tael earnings to cover its own upstream costs — compute, other paid APIs, Tael-wrapped tools it consumes — or to scale capacity. It repays out of ongoing Tael revenue; on-time repayment ramps the limit up, more capacity serves more, earns more Tael revenue, and the line grows.

This is revenue-based financing for AI agents, where Tael is the point-of-sale and Fianza is the lender reading the till.

The lending side falls out for free: a Tael operator with surplus revenue is a natural lender into Fianza vaults — earning yield on the very USDC Tael just settled for them, redeployed as credit to other agents in the same economy. The money never leaves the Tael/Fianza loop.

ASK

The ask to Tael

Technical — unblocks Angle B
  • Add a memo/tag convention to settlement payments so a transfer is attributable to Tael, and ideally to a resource/invocation, from on-chain data alone — today they're bare Operation.payment transfers with no marker.
  • Add amount and asset to SettlementReceipt — currently only txHash, payer, network, settledAt.
  • Hardening, not a blocker for us: a txHash uniqueness constraint — there's no app-level replay or double-spend protection today.
Collaboration
  • A testnet Tael-wrapped endpoint we can point the demo agent at.
  • Willingness to accept a Fianza PaymentVerifier as a documented, first-class verifier option alongside mock and Stellar.
  • Mainnet coordination when Tael wires it up — it's testnet-only today, on a self-issued test USDC rather than Circle's canonical asset.
  • Willingness to co-document: Tael docs reference Fianza as the buyer-side credit client and seller-side financing layer; Fianza docs reference Tael as a first-class revenue source and paid-service ecosystem.
